An Agentic Chatbot for Personalized and Real-Time Restaurant
Services
Chapter 1 – Introduction
1.1 Introduction
The global restaurant industry has experienced substantial digital transformation over the past
decade. The rapid expansion of smartphone usage, cloud computing technologies, and artificial
intelligence applications has significantly influenced customer expectations and service delivery
models. Modern consumers increasingly depend on digital platforms to discover restaurants,
review menus, evaluate customer ratings, confirm availability, make reservations, and complete
secure payments.International platforms such as OpenTable, Uber Eats, Google Maps, and
TripAdvisor have reshaped restaurant customer interaction by providing centralized digital
services. These systems demonstrate the growing importance of digital engagement within the
global food service sector.
The trend of digital transformation is equally visible across Asian countries. Nations such as
Singapore, India, and South Korea have adopted mobile-based reservation platforms, QR-code
payment systems, and integrated food delivery applications. High mobile penetration rates and
urbanization have accelerated the demand for efficient digital restaurant services in these regions.
Despite this technological progress, many restaurant-related functions continue to operate in
fragmented systems. Restaurant discovery, availability verification, reservation confirmation, and
payment processing are often handled through separate platforms.
Personalization mechanisms are generally limited to static filtering rather than intelligent
recommendation models that adapt to user preferences. This fragmentation limits the overall
efficiency of digital restaurant ecosystems.
The digital restaurant landscape in Sri Lanka reflects similar patterns, but at a comparatively early
stage of technological integration. Internet usage and smartphone adoption have increased
steadily, encouraging restaurants to establish online visibility through social media and webbased platforms. However, reservation management frequently depends on manual coordination
through phone calls or messaging applications. Real-time synchronization between online listings
and actual table availability remains limited. Menu updates may not always reflect current stock
or availability, creating inconsistencies between digital information and physical operations.
These operational gaps create inconvenience for customers and increase administrative
workload for restaurant staff.
Existing chatbot systems have been introduced in several industries to enhance digital
communication. Most of these systems operate using rule-based logic and predefined response
structures. Such implementations primarily respond to direct user inputs without the capacity for
autonomous task coordination, contextual memory retention, or multi-step reasoning.Consequently, these systems are unable to integrate multiple restaurant service functions into a
unified conversational workflow. The need therefore exists for a more advanced conversational
system capable of intelligent coordination and real-time integration of restaurant services.
This study proposes the development of an Agentic AI Chatbot Platform designed to integrate
restaurant discovery, personalization, real-time availability management, reservation processing,
and secure payment handling within a single conversational interface tailored to the Sri Lankan
context.
1.2 Background of the Study
The increasing digitization of the global restaurant industry has created both opportunities and
structural challenges. Industry analyses indicate that online reservation and food service
technology markets continue to expand due to rising digital payment adoption and consumer
preference for convenient booking systems. A significant proportion of customers now prefer
online reservation mechanisms over traditional phone-based bookings because of time efficiency
and ease of access. However, current digital infrastructures often treat discovery, reservation,
and payment functions as independent modules rather than components of a continuous
workflow.
This structural separation results in fragmented customer experiences. A typical reservation
process requires users to search for restaurants on one platform, examine reviews or menus on
another interface, confirm availability separately, and complete payment through an external
gateway. Each additional step increases cognitive effort and time consumption. Research in
digital service management demonstrates that fragmented workflows negatively influence
customer satisfaction and service reliability. Inconsistent information across platforms may further
contribute to user frustration and reduced trust in digital systems.
Operational challenges are equally significant for restaurant service providers. Manual updates
of menu items, table availability adjustments, and reservation confirmations increase
administrative complexity. In environments where real-time synchronization is absent,
overbooking and underutilization of resources become common. These inefficiencies reduce
operational productivity and may lead to financial losses. Within Sri Lanka, small and mediumscale restaurants frequently rely on semi-manual coordination methods, highlighting the absence
of integrated digital management solutions.
Conversational AI technologies have been implemented in various sectors to improve service
interaction. However, most chatbot systems rely on rule-based architectures that lack
autonomous task delegation, contextual reasoning, and adaptive personalization. These
limitations prevent effective coordination between discovery, reservation, and payment
processes. Recent developments in agent-based artificial intelligence architectures suggest that
multi-agent coordination frameworks can address these shortcomings by enabling specialized
agents to manage distinct tasks under a unified orchestration mechanism.
Despite advancements in agentic AI research, limited studies have examined its application within
integrated restaurant service management systems, particularly in developing digital marketssuch as Sri Lanka. This gap establishes the foundation for the present research, which seeks to
design and evaluate a unified agent-driven conversational platform capable of improving service
efficiency, personalization, and real-time operational synchronization.
1.3 Research Questions and Objectives
1.3.1 Research Questions
This study is guided by the following research questions:
1. How can an agentic multi-agent architecture improve the integration of restaurant
discovery, reservation management, and payment processing within a single
conversational system?
2. How can real-time synchronization between digital platforms and physical restaurant
operations be achieved through conversational interaction?
3. How can personalization be enhanced using intelligent intent detection and user
preference modeling?
4. What system architecture ensures scalability, security, and practical feasibility within the
Sri Lankan restaurant industry?
1.3.2 Overall Aim
The overall aim of this research is to design and develop an intelligent agentic chatbot platform
that integrates restaurant discovery, real-time table availability, personalized recommendations,
reservation management, and secure payment processing into a unified conversational interface.
1.3.3 Specific Objectives
1. To design a multi-agent architecture coordinated through a central orchestration
mechanism.
2. To integrate restaurant discovery, availability verification, reservation handling, and
payment processing into a single conversational workflow.
3. To implement real-time synchronization of table availability and menu updates.
4. To develop semantic intent recognition for accurate task routing.
5. To incorporate personalized recommendation features based on user preferences.
6. To ensure secure transaction handling and role-based access control for customers and
service providers.1.4 Significance, Scope, and Structure of the Thesis
1.4.1 Significance of the Study
This research contributes to both academic knowledge and practical industry development. From
an academic perspective, the study demonstrates the application of agentic AI architecture within
a real-world service domain. The integration of multi-agent coordination with conversational AI
extends beyond traditional rule-based chatbot implementations.
From an industry perspective, the system addresses operational inefficiencies within the Sri
Lankan restaurant sector. The proposed platform enhances workflow integration, reduces manual
coordination, improves personalization, and ensures real-time synchronization between digital
and physical operations. The research therefore provides a scalable and practical technological
solution tailored to local industry requirements.
1.4.2 Scope of the Study
• The scope of this research includes:
• Development of a conversational chatbot platform
• Implementation of multi-agent orchestration architecture
• Real-time reservation and availability management
• Secure payment confirmation integration
• Role-based system access for customers and restaurant administrators
• System performance and usability evaluation
The study does not include logistics management, food delivery tracking, or integration with
external marketplace delivery services beyond reservation and payment handling.

This literature review examines existing research related to chatbot systems, restaurant
recommendation platforms, digital ordering technologies, conversational recommender systems,
and agent-based architectures. The purpose is to analyze how prior studies have addressed
restaurant service automation and to identify research gaps relevant to the proposed Agentic AI
Chatbot for Real-Time Restaurant Discovery and Reservation Management.
Research on chatbot usage in restaurant services demonstrates measurable improvements in
service efficiency. Leung and Wen [1] found that chatbot systems enhance order processing
speed and reduce waiting time in takeout services. However, the study revealed that customers
prefer human assistance for complex and personalized interactions, indicating that rule-based
chatbots lack contextual reasoning and adaptive intelligence.
The distinction between traditional AI agents and agentic AI systems has been conceptually
examined in [2]. The study explains that conventional AI agents perform isolated predefined tasks,
whereas agentic AI systems exhibit autonomous goal-oriented reasoning and dynamic
coordination. This distinction is fundamental to the proposed research, which aims to move
beyond reactive chatbot behavior.
Several implementation-focused studies explore restaurant ordering platforms. The “Foody”
smart restaurant management system integrates AI-driven ordering and recommendation
capabilities [3]. Similarly, the digital transformation study on real-time innovation in dining
environments identifies operational inefficiencies such as long waiting times and limited seating
coordination [4]. Both systems enhance automation but lack integrated conversational
orchestration across multiple service components.
Kim et al. [5] proposed an AI chatbot-based mobile application for restaurant recommendations.
The system improves personalization through preference-based filtering; however, it operates
independently from reservation and payment systems. Worth Eat, introduced in [11], similarly
focuses on intelligent recommendation algorithms without integrating full restaurant workflow
management.
Conversational recommender systems have been comprehensively surveyed in [12]. The study
emphasizes that dialogue-based recommendation improves user satisfaction by dynamically
eliciting preferences. Nevertheless, most conversational recommender systems are limited to
suggestion mechanisms and do not integrate operational management such as reservation
coordination or payment processing.
Canteeno [7] and the Menu card Chatbot [8] present chatbot-based restaurant ordering systems
that improve digital menu interaction and order automation. These systems operate within rulebased logic and predefined flows. Research on chatbot user experience [9] highlights that
usability, contextual continuity, and response reliability significantly influence user trust and
system adoption. The study on AI chatbot adoption for service enhancement [13] further confirms
that chatbot systems positively impact service quality when reliability and transparency are
ensured.Digital ordering innovations have also been explored through multi-touch and interactive dining
systems. Cheong [10] demonstrates that digital self-ordering systems improve operational
efficiency and reduce dependency on manual processes. Restaurant analytics research [14]
emphasizes data-driven decision-making to enhance operational performance and customer
engagement. However, these systems typically function independently from conversational AI
frameworks.
The study on inconsistent reservation processes [15] shows that inefficiencies in booking
management negatively affect customer satisfaction and operational reliability. This finding
reinforces the need for real-time synchronization between digital platforms and physical
restaurant operations.
Multi-agent system research provides a theoretical foundation for coordinated autonomous
architectures. The integration of software agents [16] outlines recommended practices for agent
communication and distributed task handling. Studies on distributed circle formation control [6]
and finite-time tracking consensus control [17] demonstrate coordinated behavior among multiple
agents in dynamic environments. Although these studies focus on control systems, they establish
principles of distributed coordination relevant to agentic AI design.
The UniGen framework [18] introduces unified modeling of initial agent states and task generation
mechanisms. This research highlights structured task delegation and state management in agentbased systems, reinforcing the feasibility of multi-agent orchestration.
Despite extensive research in individual domains, a consistent limitation across studies is system
fragmentation. Chatbots focus on interaction, recommendation systems focus on personalization,
analytics systems focus on data insights, and multi-agent research focuses on theoretical
coordination. Integrated conversational architectures that unify discovery, recommendation,
reservation, real-time synchronization, and payment processing remain underexplored,
particularly within developing digital ecosystems.
Therefore, a significant research gap exists in designing and implementing a unified agentic AI
chatbot platform capable of coordinating multiple restaurant service functions through a multiagent architecture. The proposed study addresses this gap by integrating conversational
intelligence, real-time synchronization, graph-based personalization, and agentic orchestration
within a scalable restaurant service ecosystem.References

3.1 Research and Development Approach
This study adopts an Agile-driven research and development methodology to design and
implement the proposed agentic AI chatbot platform. The project involves both conceptual
research and practical system development, requiring continuous refinement based on user
needs, restaurant operational requirements, and AI system performance. Therefore, an iterative
and adaptive methodology is more appropriate than a rigid linear development model.
Agile methodology supports incremental system development through short development cycles
known as sprints. Each sprint allows the implementation, testing, and refinement of specific
system components. This approach ensures that functional improvements can be introduced
progressively while maintaining flexibility to adapt to newly identified requirements. Since the
proposed system integrates conversational AI, multi-agent coordination, real-time data
synchronization, and payment processing, continuous validation is essential to ensure technical
reliability and usability.
The methodological framework integrates three major perspectives: system engineering
principles for architectural planning, user-centered design for ensuring usability and stakeholder
alignment, and iterative software engineering practices for implementation. This combined
approach ensures that the final solution is technically robust, scalable, and aligned with real-world
restaurant service requirements.
3.2 Methodology Phases
The development process is structured into six interconnected phases. Each phase contributes
to the systematic design, implementation, and evaluation of the agentic chatbot platform.
3.2.1 Problem Identification and Requirement Analysis
The first phase focuses on identifying the core problem and defining system requirements. This
phase establishes the foundation for the proposed solution by analyzing the limitations of existing
restaurant platforms at global, regional, and local levels.
A comprehensive literature review was conducted to examine restaurant service platforms,
conversational AI systems, agentic chatbot architectures, and recommendation mechanisms.
Existing digital systems operating globally, across Asia, and within Sri Lanka were analyzed to
understand architectural limitations and service fragmentation. In addition, surveys and informal
interviews were conducted with restaurant providers and potential customers to gather insights
into operational inefficiencies and user challenges.
The analysis revealed several recurring issues, including fragmented service workflows, limited
personalization, delayed availability updates, and inefficient reservation handling. Based on these
findings, functional and non-functional requirements were defined. Functional requirements
include restaurant discovery, personalized recommendations, reservation processing, real-time
table management, pre-ordering, and secure payment handling. Non-functional requirementsinclude system scalability, security, performance efficiency, usability, and reliability. User stories
were formulated for three primary roles: customers, restaurant providers, and administrators.
The output of this phase consisted of a structured requirement specification document and a
prioritized product backlog to guide iterative development.
3.2.2 System Design and Architecture
Following requirement analysis, the system architecture was designed to address the identified
challenges. The architectural design emphasizes modularity, scalability, and multi-agent
coordination.
The proposed system consists of four primary layers: the frontend layer, the agentic AI layer, the
data-layer, and the integration layer. The frontend layer includes the chatbot interface developed
for user interaction. The agentic AI layer comprises a central orchestration agent and multiple
specialized agents responsible for discovery, recommendation, reservation management,
payment coordination, and notification handling. The data layer integrates Firebase Firestore for
real-time operational data and Neo4j graph database for personalized recommendation modeling.
The integration layer connects external services such as payment gateways and notification
systems.
To ensure clarity and structural consistency, use case diagrams, activity diagrams, and sequence
diagrams were developed. These diagrams illustrate interactions between users, agents, and
system components. User stories defined during the previous phase were mapped to
corresponding agents and workflows. This architectural planning ensures coordinated task
delegation and contextual continuity within the conversational interface.
The output of this phase included a detailed system architecture model, agent responsibility
definitions, database schemas, and an orchestration plan for agent communication.
3.2.3 Iterative Development Using Agile Sprints
The implementation phase was conducted using Agile sprints. Each sprint focused on delivering
a specific set of prioritized features from the product backlog.
Sprint planning sessions were conducted to define sprint goals and allocate development tasks.
Early sprints focused on developing the chatbot interface and basic restaurant discovery
functionality. Subsequent sprints implemented the recommendation agent, reservation
management agent, payment integration, and notification services.
The frontend chatbot interface was developed using React and Expo Go to ensure cross-platform
compatibility. Backend services were implemented using Node.js and Spring Boot to manage API
communication and business logic. The agentic AI layer was developed using Python and
FastAPI, integrating LangChain and Google Vertex AI (Gemini) for language model processingand intent detection. Firebase Firestore was used for real-time data storage, while Neo4j graph
database supported personalized recommendation modeling based on user preferences and
relationship mapping.
At the end of each sprint, review and retrospective sessions were conducted. Functional testing
was performed to validate system components, and stakeholder feedback was collected to refine
requirements. This iterative process ensured continuous improvement and alignment with project
objectives.
3.2.4 Testing and Evaluation
Testing and evaluation were conducted to ensure system reliability, functionality, and usability.
Multiple testing strategies were applied to validate different system aspects.
Functional testing verified the correct operation of restaurant discovery, personalized
recommendations, reservation handling, pre-ordering, and payment processing workflows.
Performance testing measured chatbot response times and agent coordination efficiency under
varying user loads. Usability testing was conducted with selected participants to evaluate interface
clarity, conversational interaction quality, and overall user satisfaction. Integration testing ensured
seamless communication between agents, databases, and external services such as payment
gateways and notification systems.
Evaluation metrics included recommendation accuracy based on user feedback, efficiency of realtime synchronization between digital listings and restaurant operations, reservation and pre-order
success rates, and user satisfaction scores. These evaluation measures provided quantitative
and qualitative insights into system effectiveness.
3.2.5 Deployment and Real-World Integration
The final phase focused on system deployment and real-world readiness. The platform was
deployed in a controlled testing environment involving selected partner restaurants. Operational
performance and user interactions were monitored to identify potential system refinements.
Restaurant staff were provided with guidance on updating menu information, managing table
availability, and handling pre-orders through the administrative interface. Documentation was
prepared to support system maintenance and operational continuity.
The output of this phase was a functional, scalable, and user-oriented agentic chatbot platform
suitable for practical implementation within the restaurant industry.3.3 Justification for Using Agile Methodology
The selection of Agile methodology is justified by the dynamic nature of the project. The
development of a multi-agent AI system requires continuous testing, refinement, and adaptation
to emerging requirements. Agile supports iterative enhancement, stakeholder collaboration, and
progressive validation.
The iterative structure allows early detection of technical limitations and usability concerns.
Flexibility enables adaptation to changing user requirements or AI performance adjustments.
Continuous testing ensures system reliability and integration stability. Stakeholder involvement
ensures that restaurant providers and customers contribute feedback throughout the
development process.
These characteristics make Agile particularly suitable for projects that integrate artificial
intelligence components with real-world operational systems.
3.4 Summary of Methodology
This research employs an Agile-driven research and development methodology that integrates
system engineering principles, user-centered design, and iterative software development
practices. The methodology is structured into phases including problem identification, system
design, iterative implementation, testing and evaluation, and deployment preparation.
Through incremental sprints, each component of the agentic chatbot platform was designed,
implemented, validated, and refined. The multi-agent architecture enables real-time,
personalized, and intelligent restaurant service coordination, while Agile practices ensure
continuous stakeholder involvement and system validation. This structured methodological
approach supports the development of a scalable and practical solution tailored to the Sri Lankan
restaurant industry.
